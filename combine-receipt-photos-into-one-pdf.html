Combine receipt photos into one PDF

If you have a phone full of paper-receipt photos and one filing, claim or reimbursement to send, this turns them into a single PDF: one receipt per page, in date order, under a cover page that lists them and adds them up. It happens in the browser tab you already have open.

What is in the PDF

Cover index
Every receipt in date order with its date, note and amount, then a total for the batch. On a long batch the index continues on a second page.
One receipt per page
The photo is fitted to the page and centred. Nothing is cropped or cut off.
A footer on every page
Which receipt it is (4 of 9), its date and its amount — so pages survive being printed and shuffled on a desk.
A file you can email
Photos are resized to 2200 pixels on the long edge, so a stack of receipts stays a few megabytes instead of forty.

Doing it, step by step

1. Put the photos in
Drop them on the page, or click choose files. JPG, PNG, WebP and GIF. A file that is not a photo, or a photo this browser cannot read, is listed in a Skipped: line by name and the rest of the batch still builds.
2. Check the list
Each photo gets a row: a note field, a date, an amount. Where the date came out of the filename the row says so; where it could not, the date is left for you to type. Move a row with the arrows, rotate a sideways photo with the ↻ button, remove a mistake with the ×.
3. Build it
Give the PDF a title if you want one (“Client expenses, March”), pick Letter or A4, and press Build the PDF. The download link appears with the result: how many receipts, how many pages, how big, and how long it took. The same rows also come down as a CSV — one line per receipt plus the total — if the numbers are going into a spreadsheet.

Where the dates come from

From the filename, because that is usually the only place a date exists once a receipt is a photo. These all give their date up:

Anything genuinely ambiguous — receipt_03-04-2026.jpg could be 4 March or 3 April — is left blank rather than guessed, and you type it in the row. The date is what orders the batch: photos are put in date order as they load, so dropping a pile in any order still gives you a PDF that reads oldest to newest. You can still move a row by hand afterwards.

What it costs

Free for a PDF of up to 5 receipts: the cover index, the totals and the download are all included, with no account and no email. A one-time $9 unlock removes the 5-receipt limit and the small footer mark on each page. No subscription, no upsell.

The tool page states plainly whether checkout is connected yet. While it is not, nothing on this site can take money, and the free version works fully.

Why nothing gets uploaded

Receipts carry your card number, your address and what you buy. There is no server here to send them to — no backend, no API key, no analytics. The tool page carries a content-security policy whose connect-src 'none' directive blocks every network request, so it could not upload your photos even if a bug tried to. The PDF is assembled by the JavaScript already running in the tab. The full source is on GitHub.

Questions

Can I use photos from my phone?
That is the usual input. A photo taken at an angle is fine; the receipt is not cropped, so a bit of table around it does no harm.
My receipt is upside down or sideways
Use the ↻ button on its row before you build. It turns the photo 90 degrees each press and the PDF picks up the turned version.
Can I do a whole year in one PDF?
Yes, on the unlocked tier there is no limit. On the free tier the PDF is built from the first 5 receipts in the list, and the result tells you how many were left out.
Do the receipts have to be in order before I start?
No. A batch is sorted by the date each receipt carries before the PDF is built.
What does it do about tax?
Nothing. It builds one ordered PDF with a total. What you claim, and whether you can claim it, is your call and your accountant’s.
