Make a reimbursement claim PDF

A claim is a pile of receipts plus a number: whoever pays it wants each expense dated, and the whole lot added up, in one file they can open on the first try. This turns the photos on your phone into exactly that — one PDF, one receipt per page, under a cover page that lists them in date order and totals them. It happens in the browser tab you already have open.

What goes in the claim

Two things, and they fail in different ways. The first is the receipts themselves: paper fades, so a till receipt left in a drawer or a car can be unreadable within a year, which is why photographing each one when you get it is the whole trick. The second is the total — a claim needs a number on it, and adding a stack of receipts by hand is where the mistakes come from.

So the job is: one photo per receipt, a date on each, and one file that carries the list and the sum. Nothing else is needed, and nothing here needs an account.

What this tool produces

Cover index
Every receipt in date order with its date, note and amount, then a total for the batch. On a long claim the index continues onto a second page.
One receipt per page
The photo is fitted to the page and centred. Nothing is cropped or cut off, so the amounts the claim rests on are all readable in the file.
A footer on every page
Which receipt it is (4 of 9), its date and its amount — so the pages survive being printed and shuffled on someone’s desk.
A CSV of the same rows
The index rows also download as a CSV — one line per receipt plus the total — so the numbers open straight in a spreadsheet.
A file you can email
Photos are resized to 2200 pixels on the long edge, so a stack of receipts stays a few megabytes instead of forty.

Doing it, step by step

1. Put the photos in
Drop them on the tool page, or click choose files. JPG, PNG, WebP and GIF. A file that is not a photo is named in a Skipped: line and the rest of the batch still builds.
2. Check the list
Each photo gets a row: a note, a date, an amount. Where the date came out of the filename the row says so; where it could not, you type it in. Move a row with the arrows, turn a sideways photo with the ↻ button, remove a mistake with the ×.
3. Add up and download
Give the file a title if you want one (“Travel, October”), pick Letter or A4, and press Build the PDF. The result shows the receipt count, the page count, the size and the total, then gives you the download.

Nothing gets uploaded

A claim spreadsheet is a list of everything you bought, and the receipt photos carry your card number and your address. There is no server here to send them to — no backend, no API key, no analytics. The tool page carries a content-security policy whose connect-src 'none' directive blocks every network request, so it could not upload your photos even if a bug tried to. The PDF and the CSV are assembled by the JavaScript already running in the tab, and land in your own downloads folder. The full source is on GitHub.

What it costs

Free for a file of up to 5 receipts: the cover index, the totals, the PDF, the CSV and the download are all included, with no account and no email. Above that, the tool page states what the unlock is and whether checkout is connected yet — while it is not, nothing on this site can take money and the free version works fully.

What this does not do

It does not decide what you can claim, does not compute a rate or a per-diem, and does not submit anything anywhere. It puts your receipts in one ordered file with a total. Whether an expense is reimbursable, what evidence is wanted, and how long to keep the file are questions for whoever pays the claim — not for this page.

The tool itself makes no such claim either. It builds the file. That is all.

Questions

Can I use this for a work expense claim?
Yes. It builds one PDF with every receipt in date order, its date and amount listed on a cover index, and a total for the batch. What an employer or a client will accept is their rule, not this page’s.
Do my receipts get sent to a website?
No. There is no upload and no server to upload to. The page ships a content-security policy that blocks every network request, so the photos are read and the PDF is assembled in your own browser tab.
Can I get the numbers into a spreadsheet?
Yes. After a build, the same rows download as a CSV — one line per receipt plus the total — so they open straight in Excel, Numbers or Sheets.
How many receipts can I put in one file?
Free for a PDF of up to 5 receipts, cover index and totals included, with no account and no email. The result tells you how many receipts were left out.
Does it work out what I can claim?
No. It builds the file. Which expenses are reimbursable, and at what rate, is between you and whoever pays the claim.
