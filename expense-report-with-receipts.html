Make an expense report with the receipts attached

An expense report is two things handed over together: a list of what you spent — dated, with a total — and the receipts that back it up. This turns the photos of those receipts into exactly that file: one PDF whose first page lists every expense in date order with its amount and a total for the batch, followed by each receipt on its own page. It happens in the browser tab you already have open.

A report is a list plus the paper behind it

The words “expense report” hide a simple shape: rows of what you spent, each with a date and an amount, a total at the bottom, and the receipts those rows describe. The rows without the receipts are just an assertion; the receipts without the rows are a shoebox. Whoever the report goes to — an employer, a client, an accountant — wants both in one file they can open on the first try.

This page is not a blank template you fill in by hand. It is the filled version: you supply the receipts as photos, and the tool lists them, dates them, and adds them up. The report is the first page it builds.

What the report contains

Cover index (the report itself)
Every receipt in date order with its date, note and amount, then a total for the batch. On a long report the index continues onto a second page.
One receipt per page
Each photo is fitted to the page and centred. Nothing is cropped or cut off, so every amount the report rests on is readable in the file.
A footer on every page
Which receipt it is (4 of 9), its date and its amount — so the pages survive being printed and shuffled on someone’s desk.
The same rows as a CSV
The index rows also download as a CSV — one line per receipt plus the total — so the list opens straight in a spreadsheet if the report is wanted that way.
A file you can email
Photos are resized to 2200 pixels on the long edge, so a stack of receipts stays a few megabytes instead of forty.

Doing it, step by step

1. Put the photos in
Drop them on the tool page, or click choose files. JPG, PNG, WebP and GIF. A file that is not a photo is named in a Skipped: line and the rest of the batch still builds.
2. Check the list
Each photo gets a row: a note, a date, an amount. Where the date came out of the filename the row says so; where it could not, you type it in. Move a row with the arrows, turn a sideways photo with the ↻ button, remove a mistake with the ×.
3. Add up and download
Give the file a title if you want one (“Travel, October”), pick Letter or A4, and press Build the PDF. The result shows the receipt count, the page count, the size and the total, then gives you the download.

Nothing gets uploaded

An expense report is a list of everything you bought, and the receipt photos carry your card number and your address. There is no server here to send them to — no backend, no API key, no analytics. The tool page carries a content-security policy whose connect-src 'none' directive blocks every network request, so it could not upload your photos even if a bug tried to. The PDF and the CSV are assembled by the JavaScript already running in the tab, and land in your own downloads folder. The full source is on GitHub.

What it costs

Free for a file of up to 5 receipts: the cover index, the totals, the PDF, the CSV and the download are all included, with no account and no email. Above that, the tool page states what the unlock is and whether checkout is connected yet — while it is not, nothing on this site can take money and the free version works fully.

What this does not do

It does not decide what you can claim back, does not compute a rate or a per-diem, and does not submit anything anywhere. It puts your receipts in one ordered file with a total. Whether an expense is reimbursable, what evidence is wanted, and how the report has to be laid out are questions for whoever it goes to — not for this page.

The tool itself makes no such claim either. It builds the file. That is all.

Questions

Is this an expense report template?
It is the filled version rather than a blank one. You bring the receipts; the tool lists them in date order, with the amount and a note for each, on a cover page that totals the batch, and attaches every receipt behind it. The cover page is the expense report — its rows are the lines, its total is the total.
Can I get the expense list into a spreadsheet?
Yes. After a build, the same rows download as a CSV — one line per receipt plus the total — so they open straight in Excel, Numbers or Sheets if the report is wanted as a spreadsheet instead of a PDF.
Do my receipts get sent to a website?
No. There is no upload and no server to upload to. The page ships a content-security policy that blocks every network request, so the photos are read and the PDF is assembled in your own browser tab.
How many receipts can I put in one report?
Free for a PDF of up to 5 receipts, cover index and totals included, with no account and no email. The result tells you how many receipts were left out.
Does it decide what I can claim back?
No. It builds the file. Which expenses are reimbursable, and at what rate, is between you and whoever pays the report.
